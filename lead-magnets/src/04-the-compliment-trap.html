The Compliment Trap · A 10-minute post-call debrief

Was that interview
good, or did they
just like you?

Do this within ten minutes of hanging up, before you message your co-founder “they loved it”. That is exactly when a compliment quietly turns into “they’d pay”, and the notes never get written.

10 minutesOne sheet per callPrint five copies

Everything they said goes in one of four bins

Compliment

Praise for you or your idea.

“That’s a really cool idea.”

WORTH: NOTHING. IT COST THEM NOTHING TO SAY.

Fluff

Generic claims, hypotheticals, “I would”, “I usually”, “people like me”.

“I’d definitely use something like that.”

WORTH: NOTHING. IT’S A FORECAST.

Fact

Something that already happened: a specific time, workaround, cost or person.

“Last March I lost two days rebuilding a client’s invoice history.”

WORTH: REAL. THIS IS DATA.

Commitment

They gave up something now: time, reputation or money.

“Let me introduce you to our finance lead.”

WORTH: MOST. THIS IS EVIDENCE.

The idea that a compliment is worth nothing, and a commitment of time, reputation or money is worth a lot, comes from Rob Fitzpatrick’s The Mom Test. This sheet just makes you sort the pile while it’s still warm.

“they loved it”
is not a finding.

UNASSUMED.Reading your sheet

What your ticks are telling you.

These aren’t scores. They point at what to change before the next call.

Mostly compliments

You probably pitched. Once your idea is in the room, people react to it politely. Next time, hold the solution back until the last five minutes, or leave it out entirely.

Mostly fluff

You asked about the future. “Would you…” and “how often do you…” get you forecasts. Swap them for “tell me about the last time…”. Field sheet 01 drills exactly this.

Facts, no commitment

A good conversation that didn’t go anywhere. You learned something real. Next time, ask for a next step before you hang up (see the ladder below). A “no” is information too.

At least one commitment

That’s evidence. Write it in your assumption list with the date and who it was. One commitment from a stranger outweighs a pile of compliments from friends.

The commitment ladder: ask for one rung before you hang up

Time
  • A follow-up call with a clear purpose
  • Looking at a rough sketch with you
  • Trying a manual version for a week
Reputation
  • An introduction to a colleague with the same problem
  • An intro to whoever signs off the spending
Money
  • A pre-order or deposit
  • A letter of intent
  • Paying for the manual version

The time, reputation and money categories follow The Mom Test. Reputation-type asks usually fit B2B better than consumer products.

Find the compliment-bait before the call.

This sheet tells you after the call that a question invited a compliment. Unassumed lets you find out before: rehearse against a synthetic customer who objects, digresses and won’t be led, then see which of your questions did the leading. It’s practice, not proof. Only a real customer is evidence.
