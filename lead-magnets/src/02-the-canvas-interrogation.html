UNASSUMED.01 · Interrogate every box

Ask each box one hard question.

Write your guess as a sentence a stranger could prove wrong: We believe [who] [does / has / will pay for what], because [what we’ve actually seen]. If the “because” is empty, that tells you something too.

BoxThe question to ask itOur guess, as a sentence someone could disprove
WILL ANYONE WANT IT? · START HERE
Customer segmentsWHOCan I name three real people who had this problem in the last month?
Value propositionWHY THEMWhat do they do about it today, and what does that cost them?
ChannelsHOW THEY FIND USWhere did the last person with this problem go looking for help?
Customer relationshipsHOW WE KEEP THEMIs this a one-off problem or a recurring one? How do I know?
Revenue streamsWHO PAYS, HOWWhat have they already paid to solve it, and who signed off?
CAN WE DELIVER IT? · THEN THESE
Key activitiesWHAT WE MUST DO WELLWhat’s the one thing that, done badly, makes the rest pointless?
Key resourcesWHAT WE NEEDWhat do we need that we don’t yet have, and how sure are we we can get it?
Key partnersWHO WE DEPEND ONWho would have to say yes, and have we asked them?
CAN IT PAY? · THEN THIS
Cost structureWHAT IT COSTSWhat does it cost us to win and serve one customer? Is that a guess?

Why this order? If nobody wants it, nothing else on the canvas matters, so test desirability (“will anyone want it?”) before feasibility and viability. Number each guess 1–9 for the next page.

Order is a rule of thumb

Prioritising by importance and evidence follows the assumptions-mapping approach in Bland & Osterwalder’s Testing Business Ideas.

What counts as evidence? Score honestly before you plot

NoneA compliment“Love it!” costs them nothing.
NoneA forecast“I’d use that”, “people would pay”.
WeakA past storyA specific time it happened to them.
GoodA workaroundTime or money they already spend on it.
StrongA commitmentTime, an intro, a deposit, now.

Your own conviction isn’t on the ladder. Neither is your mum’s.

A starter menu of cheap testsillustrative · roughly cheapest first

Dig for workaroundsSearch forums and reviews for people already hacking a fix or paying for one.
Five conversationsPast-behaviour interviews with people from your segment. See field sheets 01 & 03.
Do it by handDeliver the outcome manually for one or two people before you build anything.
A one-page offerDescribe it plainly with a clear ask, and count who takes the next step.
Ask for commitmentA deposit, letter of intent or intro. The strongest signal, and the scariest ask.

Does this way of working actually help? In a randomised trial, early-stage founders trained to frame ideas as hypotheses and test them dropped weak ideas sooner and earned more revenue than a control group. A larger replication also found benefits. Camuffo, Cordova, Gambardella & Spina, Management Science (2020); replication in Strategic Management Journal (2024).

UNASSUMED.04 · Use AI honestly

Five prompts that find guesses
instead of flattering them.

Illustrative · adapt freely

Chatbots are useful for spotting what you’re assuming, and bad at telling you whether you’re right. Ask the first kind of question, never the second. Paste your canvas from page 2 where it says […].

1

Surface the guesses

Here is my business model canvas: […]. List every assumption hidden in it as a sentence that a stranger could prove false. Do not tell me whether the idea is good.
2

Make them falsifiable

For each assumption, describe what I would actually observe in the world if it were false. Be concrete: who does what, how often.
3

Argue the other side

Act as a sceptical investor who has seen 500 ideas like this fail. Which three assumptions, if wrong, would end this business? Give the strongest case against each.
4

Design the cheapest test

Design the cheapest test of this assumption that I can run in seven days without building a product: […]. State in advance the result that would mean I'm wrong.
5

Check your questions

Here are the questions I plan to ask in customer interviews: […]. Flag any that are leading, hypothetical, or mention my solution, and rewrite them to ask about specific past behaviour.

What a chatbot can’t tell you

Whether anyone will pay. Researchers who compared language-model answers with a real national survey found them similar on average, but analyses of the synthetic answers often reached different conclusions from the human data.

Bisbee et al. (2024), “Synthetic Replacements for Human Survey Data? The Perils of Large Language Models”, Political Analysis 32.

Use AI to find guesses.
Use people to test them.
Never ask a chatbot if your idea is good.

Paper doesn’t answer back

From scribble to evidence, without the theatre.

Unassumed does this loop with you. A note, voice memo or napkin photo becomes a first-pass canvas you can argue with. Every box becomes an assumption with a status. You rehearse the conversations that could break it against a synthetic customer who won’t be led, then log what real people actually said. No idea score. No “validated” stamp. Just what has happened to each belief, who said it, and when.
