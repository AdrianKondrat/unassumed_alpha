UNASSUMED.01 · Learn the Three Tells

A question can give its answer away
in three ways. We call them the Three Tells.

Start from first principles. An interview is a measurement. You want to measure what a person has actually done, because that is the closest thing to what they will do. Each Tell swaps that measurement for something cheaper: your opinion, their guess, or their politeness.

Tell 1 · Leading

The answer you want is inside the question.

It tells them which reply is correct. Most people are kind to a nervous founder, so they pick it.

“Don’t you hate chasing late invoices?”

TEST → Could a polite stranger see which answer I’m hoping for?

Tell 2 · Hypothetical

It asks for a forecast, not a memory.

“Would you”, “how much would you pay”, “do you think people…”. Asking someone to predict their own behaviour gets you an optimistic guess.

“If this existed, how often would you use it?”

TEST → Is it about the future, or about other people?

Tell 3 · Solution-biased

Your idea has walked into the room.

Once they know what you’re building, they review your idea instead of describing their life, and they are polite about it.

“Our tool uses AI to auto-chase clients. Useful?”

TEST → Does it mention my product, feature or idea?

No Tells · Clean

ask for a memory, get a story

“Tell me about the last time that happened.”  A clean question asks about a specific past event. It gets you a date, a workaround, a cost and a person: things that happened, not things they imagine. Then keep going: What did you do next? What did that cost you? What have you tried?

What the research actually saysand how sure to be about it

People say they’d pay more than they actually do. A meta-analysis comparing stated and real-money valuations found stated amounts were higher, with a median ratio of about 1.35×; other reviews report larger gaps.

Murphy, Allen, Stevens & Weatherhead (2005), Environmental & Resource Economics 30(3).

Direction: solid
Size: varies a lot

Changing what people intend changes what they do much less. Across 47 experiments, shifting intentions produced a noticeably smaller shift in behaviour, and the gap grew with time.

Webb & Sheeran (2006), Psychological Bulletin 132(2).

Replicated
Health & social behaviour, not purchases

One word can move the answer. People who were asked how fast cars “smashed” into each other gave higher speed estimates than people asked how fast they “contacted”.

Loftus & Palmer (1974), Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior 13(5).

Classic lab study
An analogy for interviews

UNASSUMED.02 · Answer key

The key, and why.

If you disagree with a call, good: argue it out loud. The reasoning matters more than the tick.

TellsWhy, and a cleaner version
01L H SPitches the app, asks for a forecast, and “would you use” has one polite answer. → “Tell me about the last time you had to find a receipt.”
02CleanA specific past event. Follow with: “What did you do? How long did it take?”
03L“Don’t you hate…” tells them what to feel. → “Walk me through what happens when a client pays late.”
04H SStated prices run higher than real ones (see p.2). → “What have you paid for anything that helps with this? What do you pay now?”
05CleanPast, specific, no idea mentioned. Dig into the workaround they describe.
06L SA pitch with a question mark at the end. Hold the solution back until you’ve heard the problem in their words.
07H S“If this existed” is both a hypothetical and your product. → “How often did this come up last month?”
08CleanOpen and about their world. It’s one of YC’s suggested openers. Follow up with “When did that last happen?” to get from opinion to a story.
09L S“Wouldn’t it be…” is the leading form, and auto-sending invoices is your feature in disguise.
10CleanGold. What they have already tried, paid for or hacked together is the strongest signal that the problem is real.
11HAsks them to speculate about other people. → “Who else have you talked to about this? What did they do?”
12LAssumes it is painful and asks for an opinion score. → “How long did invoicing take you last month?”
